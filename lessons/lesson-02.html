Lesson 02 — Guess What Happened?

Past continuous questions, past simple vs. past continuous in a story, and how to tell a short anecdote in English.

Grammar: Past continuous questions Grammar: Past simple & continuous together Speaking: Telling an anecdote Everyday English: reacting to a story

Key words for today

New vocabulary before we start:
  • anecdote — a short, funny or interesting story about something that happened
  • embarrassed — feeling shy or ashamed because of something you did
  • bump into (someone) — hit someone by accident, or meet someone by chance
  • trainers — sports shoes
  • hoodie — a sweatshirt with a hood
  • icy — covered with ice, very slippery
  • pick up — lift something from the ground
  • interrupt — stop someone while they are speaking
  • shopping centre — a large building with many shops inside

Grammar: past simple and past continuous

3. Complete the examples with the correct form of the verb in brackets.
When Clayton jumped into the water, he (wear) his wedding clothes.

While the photographer (prepare) his camera, Clayton noticed three children.
Get it right!
We can use when, while and as + past continuous for a longer action in progress.
We use when + past simple for a shorter action that interrupts a longer one.
4. Complete the text with the past simple or past continuous. Then check with your teacher.

Speaking: telling an anecdote

1. Watch video 3.4 (Everyday English). Who is the story about, and what was the person doing?
Now read the conversation and check your answers.

DAVID  Guess what happened yesterday?

LAURA  No idea.

DAVID  Well, it didn't happen to me, Adrian.

LAURA  Go on.

DAVID  He was at the new shopping centre.

LAURA  You're kidding! a shopping centre? He hates shopping.

DAVID  He was looking for new trainers.

LAURA  

DAVID  Yes, really. Now please stop interrupting. While he was looking at the trainers, someone bumped into him.

LAURA  Who was it?

DAVID  It was Neymar!

LAURA  No way!

DAVID  I know.

LAURA  Did he take a photo?

DAVID  No, he didn't. He was too embarrassed. Can you believe it?

LAURA  What a great story!

Your anecdote

Now it's your turn to tell a story — follow the four steps.
1
Plan

Think of something funny or unusual that happened — to you, a friend, or someone in your family. Make notes:

Who did it happen to?
What was the person doing when it happened?
What happened?
2
Speak

Tell your teacher the anecdote. Start with "Guess what happened?" — your teacher will react with the Everyday English phrases, so be ready to answer questions!
